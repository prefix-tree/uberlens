# UberLens: research on residual-risk evidence search

The Residual-Risk Evidence Search (RRES) proposal is honest about its sources and wrong about where its value lies. Every paper it names exists and almost every number it quotes is in the paper as stated, but six statements need correcting, and the two that matter most cut against its design: its action score V(a) is myopic value of information, which the EC²/DiRECt line it cites says "perform[s] poorly", and its "residual mass × detection probability ÷ cost" rule with a Bayes update after a miss is classical optimal search theory (Koopman 1956, Blackwell/Black 1964–65, Stone 1975), which it never cites. Its novelty is narrower than claimed as of 22 November 2026: cost-aware stopping is published (CASE, October 2026), qualitative beliefs over unseen timeline are published (ForeSeek, VESTA), and what remains open is a **coverage-based absence certificate**: turning a log of which instrument looked where, at what density, into a number for "the evidence exists but was missed". Its experiment cannot support its own go/no-go gates: **30 absent queries cannot establish a false-positive rate of 5% or below (about 60 error-free cases are needed)**, 20-query strata move five points per query, and its key control is underspecified in the direction that flatters RRES. UberLens already meets most of what the proposal asks for, because the proposal's author could not open `design.md`: always-on coverage, observations with decoded timestamps and `max_gap`, kind-based sufficiency with absence "not supportable from samples", heterogeneous instruments, a simple default search, logged decision points, grounded-success scoring and matched budgets are all there. What is new for UberLens is small and cheap: a residual-miss estimate computed from the existing coverage log, a three-way outcome, absence and near-duplicate strata sized for the gate, SAVE as a control, and output tokens charged per step. The hypothesis posterior and the value-of-information machinery should come last, behind a flag, because they are the part most exposed to prior art and to SAVE's finding that per-step deliberation is what makes a simple agent expensive.

## How the claims were checked and what stays unverified

Five research notes checked the proposal on 2026-11-22. SAVE, VideoHV-Agent, AVP, VideoAgent, VideoTree and T* were read in arXiv HTML full text, and the VideoHV-Agent, AVP, VideoAgent, VideoTree and T* repositories were cloned. The rest was read less deeply, and the report marks it where it is used. The limits are these.

The decision-theory papers (EC², Naghshvar–Javidi, DiRECt, the active-feature-acquisition papers, AdaFrame, conformal risk control, selective classification) and the search-theory sources were confirmed **from abstracts and search snippets only**; arxiv.org could not be fetched in that session. Their existence, authors, venues and abstract-level claims are verified. **Theorem statements, constants and model assumptions are recalled, not read**, and are marked **[unverified]** below. The formulas in the absence section are short Bayes-rule derivations that can be checked by hand; they are ours, not quotations.

The prior-art search was an arXiv keyword search on one day, about 60 queries. Of the papers it found, ForeSeek, VESTA, CASE, SuperMemory-VQA, VirtueBench, OVO-Bench, RevisitQA, AnswerPool, CG-Bench and AgentVidBench were read in targeted sections of the full text; **all others are abstract-only**, including the five 2026 papers set beside SAVE. "Open" in this report means "not found", not "proven absent".

Hugging Face was unreachable, so the **licence, gating, split sizes and field schema of the LV-Haystack dataset card are unverified**, as are the dataset licences of CG-Bench, VirtueBench, RevisitQA and the negative-query moment-retrieval splits. Ego4D's terms are stated from prior knowledge. The SAVE code repository could not be opened; no public code or licence is confirmed. The proposal's citation markers are opaque tokens, so each claim was checked against the paper its prose describes, not against the page its author opened.

## The sources are real, and SAVE says less than the proposal needs

### SAVE: a prior against complexity, silent on residual risk

SAVE exists: "SAVE: Less is More in Agentic Video Reasoning", arXiv 2611.15079v1, submitted 11 November 2026 by Wilk (TUM), Prokudin (ETH Zurich) and Wang (ETH Zurich, TUM), a preprint with no venue; code is promised in the future tense and none is confirmed ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15079)). The proposal's summary matches the abstract. Four things need tightening.

First, **SAVE never tested a posterior or residual-risk method.** It compares six prior agents (VideoAgent, VideoTree, VideoCuriousAgent, VideoLucy, DeepVideoDiscovery, A.I.R.) on Gemini 3 Flash and Gemma 4 across Minerva, EgoSchema and VSIBench-tiny. AVP was excluded because its released code depends on Gemini's native-video interface, and the full text never mentions VideoHV-Agent or ForeSeek ([SAVE §2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). None of the losers keeps an answer posterior, an unseen-evidence mass or a costed action score. The proposal's phrase that SAVE "argues strongly against adding hypothesis bookkeeping" overstates it: SAVE argues against unproven complexity and sets the burden of proof.

Second, **SAVE has no absent and no near-duplicate cases.** Every method is forced to answer: SAVE's prompt says "Never provide an empty or invalid answer", and the authors stopped VideoAgent from returning "unknown" so that it would match the other baselines. The metric is answer accuracy with no grounding or localization score, and the words "abstain" and "unanswerable" do not occur ([SAVE App. E.2, App. A.3.4, App. A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). Its "temporally concentrated evidence" result comes from a synthetic set of 384 videos in which a coloured square cycles continuously, and the authors say so: "Because the same event repeats throughout the video, locating the relevant event is trivial." The +35.4-point gain when an event lasts at most 1% of the video (64.6% vs 29.2%) shows that zooming resolves fine temporal structure that uniform sampling aliases. It says nothing about finding one brief event, choosing between two look-alike events, or concluding that an event is absent ([SAVE §5, App. G](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). The proposal uses this result to predict gains on exactly those cases; SAVE does not support that prediction.

Third, **the right control is SAVE proper, not SAVE's single-frame baseline.** The paper contains three systems of its own. The "Active Exploration Baseline" asks for one frame by index, starts without having seen any frame, and scores 52.8 on Minerva with Gemini, 12 points below native inference (64.8). SAVE replaces the single frame with a range query that the harness samples, and reaches 66.4 at 40% of native's cost ($32.04 vs $80.54 for the whole evaluation). SAOS, SAVE limited to one visual query, scores 57.5 and beats all six prior agents on Minerva at $13.12 ([SAVE Tables 1–3, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). "The simple baseline stays competitive" is true against other agents only. The proposal's fourth comparison system, "a SAVE-like minimal iterative baseline", should be specified as SAVE itself (range queries, up to 30 queries, at most 40 frames per query, resolution set by span width, the published prompt), with a one-query SAOS-style control beside it. A single-frame explorer is a weak control and would flatter RRES. The proposal's own null hypothesis, that strong retrieval plus one zoom is as good as sequential decision making, is close to what SAOS already showed.

Fourth, two SAVE findings bear on design choices the proposal takes for granted. **Harness-set density beat model-chosen density**: the variant where the model picked granularity scored 62.7 on Minerva and 51.3 on VSIBench; fixing frame count and resolution from the span width gave 66.4 and 57.1 ([SAVE Table 2, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). RRES has the policy choose sensor and resolution at every step. And **per-step output tokens are costly**: the single-frame baseline uses fewer total tokens per Minerva sample than SAVE (33.6K vs 39.7K) but costs more ($38.08 vs $32.04), because it emits 4.2K output tokens per sample against SAVE's 1.5K, and thinking tokens are billed as output ([SAVE Table 3, App. A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). RRES adds candidate generation and value scoring at each step. Its own ablation "zero reasoning charge versus actual reasoning charge" is the right test, and SAVE's data predict the charge will be material.

Two further facts change how results should be compared. SAVE measures tokens and API dollars only and excludes latency as too noisy; Whisper, scene detection and decoding are outside its accounting ([SAVE §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). The proposal's "fully loaded cost" is therefore a stricter standard, and results are comparable to SAVE's only if tokens and dollars are also reported SAVE's way. And SAVE's accuracy gain over native inference is measured against a native baseline throttled to SAVE's token budget (66.9 vs 62.0 on Minerva, with no gain under five minutes and +8.8 points at 15 minutes or more); against unthrottled native it is a tie ([SAVE §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15079v1)). The honest summary is "the same accuracy for less money on long video".

### VideoHV-Agent and AVP: accurately described, with one wrong adjective

Both papers are real and the proposal describes them correctly. VideoHV-Agent is the method in "Think, Then Verify: A Hypothesis–Verification Multi-Agent Framework for Long Video Understanding" (arXiv 2603.04977, CVPR 2026) ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04977)). AVP is "Active Video Perception: Iterative Evidence Seeking for Agentic Long Video Understanding" (arXiv 2512.05774, Salesforce AI Research and UNC), accepted as a **Findings** paper at CVPR 2026 per its repository README, not main track ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05774); [GitHub](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/ActiveVideoPerception)).

For VideoHV-Agent, every claim holds: options are rewritten as testable hypotheses, a Judge names "the minimal observation that can distinguish" them, a Verifier localizes a window from frame captions and inspects it, and failed verification triggers refinement ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04977v1)). Two qualifications. The "higher-quality visual inspection" is GPT-4o captioning of at most five frames; everything stays text-mediated, with no crop, resolution or frame-rate choice. And the proposal says VideoHV does not distinguish "evidence lies elsewhere" from "event is absent": true for absence, but its PARTIAL status (fetch more timestamps) versus NOT_VERIFIED (regenerate the clue) is a coarse version of the elsewhere-versus-wrong split. VideoHV also captions every frame at 1 fps before any reasoning, so it is not a budgeted-observation method at all, and its margins over VideoAgent2 are 0.4, 0.2 and 1.7 points on videos of mostly three minutes or less.

For AVP, the plan–observe–reflect loop, the 66 and 258 tokens per frame, the three-round limit, the 0.7 confidence threshold and the forced answer at the final round ("Reflector.ForceAnswer(Q, E) // force to give answer on final round") are all confirmed in the paper and the code ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05774); [GitHub](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/ActiveVideoPerception)). The correction: the proposal calls AVP's planning "multimodal-like". **AVP is visual-only.** It states "We do not include auxiliary subtitle for all benchmarks", and it has one action type: watch a span at a chosen frame rate and one of two resolutions. It has no audio, transcript or crop action.

The proposal misses two pieces of AVP evidence that help its case and one that hurts it. AVP's authors document the exact failure RRES targets: a 0.5 fps low-resolution scan never observed a short event, and "the reflector receives a logically consistent but incomplete evidence list and confidently outputs the wrong answer". Its threshold ablation is nearly flat (LVBench 73.2 / 74.8 / 74.8 at thresholds 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9), which supports the claim that reflector confidence is not a miss-risk estimate. But the same data show that a third round adds 0.2–0.6 points and that **76% of 50 sampled LVBench failures were observer perception errors**, against 8% planner and 16% reflector ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05774)). No selection policy fixes a perception error. RRES's gains, if any, will show on absent, near-duplicate and brief-evidence strata and on cost, not on headline multiple-choice accuracy. Neither code base can be ported: AVP is CC BY-NC 4.0, and VideoHV's README claims Apache 2.0 but the repository has no LICENSE file ([AVP GitHub](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/ActiveVideoPerception); [VideoHV GitHub](https://github.com/Haorane/VideoHV-Agent)).

### VideoAgent, VideoTree and T*: right numbers, three wrong readings

This is the Stanford VideoAgent (arXiv 2403.10517, ECCV 2024 per its README), not the HKUDS project of the same name that `design.md` section 12 says to avoid ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10517)). Every number the proposal quotes is in the paper: five uniform starting frames; rounds one to four using 5 / 7.5 / 8.4 / 9.9 frames for 53.8 / 58.6 / 60.2 / 59.8% on the EgoSchema 500-question subset; 11.8 frames and 59.6% without self-evaluation; 56.6% without segment selection ([arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.4, Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10517)). The round-three-to-four drop is 0.4 points, two questions of 500; it supports "more rounds did not help", not "more rounds hurt".

**The 1.9% CLIP cost share is for a three-minute video.** Appendix A computes it for N = 180 frames, with 0.02 s per CLIP computation, 20 s per caption (CogAgent on one A6000) and 10 s per LLM round, and calls it an upper bound; that timing configuration is also not the one behind the 60.2% result, which used EVA-CLIP-8B-plus and LaViLa ([arXiv HTML, Appendix A, Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10517)). Putting the paper's own constants into its own formula for a one-hour source at 1 fps gives about 27% (our arithmetic, not the paper's). "Cheap dense retrieval" holds for three-minute clips. For UberLens's sources of five minutes to two hours a dense embedding pass is a real cost that pays only when cached across questions, which is why `design.md` section 11 keeps the query-aware scan behind a budget gate.

VideoTree (arXiv 2405.19209, CVPR 2025, MIT) is described fairly. Two details: "best at depth three" is shown by an ablation that exists only in arXiv v1 and was dropped from the CVPR version, and the "recursive expansion" is a fixed rule (one extra level for a somewhat relevant cluster, two for a highly relevant one) with no further scoring at depth. Its best setting still captions about 62 frames of a 180-frame clip, and its hierarchy is over visual-similarity clusters, not over time ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19209); [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19209v1)).

T* (arXiv 2504.02259, CVPR 2025, MIT code) is real and the QA numbers hold: at 32 frames on LongVideoBench's 15–60 minute column, GPT-4o moves 50.5→53.1 and LLaVA-OneVision-72B 56.5→62.4 ([arXiv HTML, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02259)). The same table shows GPT-4o with 256 uniform frames at 61.6, so T* buys accuracy per frame, not a higher ceiling. Its mechanism is importance sampling over frames with an object detector as the scorer; it has no handle on speech, order or motion.

**The 2.1% temporal-F1 figure is VideoAgent's score, and every method sits at the floor.** The proposal writes that state-of-the-art selectors reach "only 2.1% temporal F1" on the "LVBench subset". On Haystack-LVBench, 8 uniform frames score 2.2, VideoAgent 2.1, the retrieval baseline 2.3–2.4, and T* itself 2.3–3.1; on Haystack-Ego4D the range is 1.6–3.4 ([arXiv HTML, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02259)). The figure shows a harsh metric (precision divides by every frame returned, at a ±5 s tolerance), not a gap that T* or any other selector closes. The subset is also built from **LongVideoBench**, not from LVBench, which `design.md` section 8.6 lists as a different benchmark. The proposal's underlying point survives on other evidence: temporal F1 near 2–3 coexists with QA accuracy moving from 45.9 to 51.0, so answer accuracy does conceal poor retrieval.

**The "query-weighted CLIP" citation is whole-video retrieval.** The sentence "a query-weighted aggregation of CLIP-like frame embeddings is already a strong long-video retrieval baseline" matches, by wording, "A CLIP-Hitchhiker's Guide to Long Video Retrieval" (Bain, Nagrani, Varol, Zisserman, arXiv 2205.08508), read as an abstract only and identified with high but not complete confidence ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08508)). That paper ranks whole videos against a caption. It does not show that frame-text similarity finds a moment inside one 90-minute source. On the within-video task, T*'s retrieval baseline is at or below uniform sampling on temporal recall (21.8 vs 24.9 at 32 frames on Haystack-LVBench), though that baseline may be detector-based; VideoAgent's CLIP-driven row reaches recall of 8.5 at 10.1 frames ([arXiv HTML, Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02259)). The retrieval baseline should stay in any experiment because it is cheap and cacheable, but it should not be called "already strong" for moment finding.

### Decision theory: the cited works support the shape of the score, not the score

All five works exist and say roughly what the proposal claims, at abstract level. EC² is Golovin, Krause and Ray, "Near-Optimal Bayesian Active Learning with Noisy Observations" (NeurIPS 2010, arXiv 1010.3091): greedy generalized binary search "can perform very poorly" with noisy observations, EC² is competitive with the optimal policy, and "the bounds still hold when the tests have non-uniform cost and their noise is correlated" ([arXiv abs](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091)). Naghshvar and Javidi, "Active sequential hypothesis testing" (Annals of Statistics 41(6), 2013), give lower bounds by dynamic programming and two heuristic policies, one asymptotically optimal ([Project Euclid](https://www.projecteuclid.org/euclid.aos/1387313387)). DiRECt is Chen et al., "Submodular Surrogates for Value of Information" (AAAI 2015) ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9694)). AdaFrame (Wu et al., CVPR 2019, arXiv 1811.12432) trains an LSTM to predict the utility of seeing more frames and matches all-frame accuracy with about 8.2–8.7 frames on FCVID and ActivityNet; the proposal correctly limits it to a precedent for a later learned component ([CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Wu_AdaFrame_Adaptive_Frame_Selection_for_Fast_Video_Recognition_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf)). The theorem bodies, constants and exact assumptions of all of these are **[unverified]**.

Three corrections follow, and they are the most important in this review.

**The proposal's V(a) is myopic value of information, which the line it cites also criticises.** V(a) is the one-step expected reduction in terminal decision loss. The proposal cites EC² "while avoiding the claim that ordinary information gain is automatically optimal". But DiRECt's abstract says that optimising value of information non-myopically is intractable and that "myopic (greedy) approximations are known to perform poorly"; the EC²/DiRECt answer is a different surrogate (cutting edges between hypotheses that lead to different decisions), not one-step value of information ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9694)). The known failure is visible in the proposal's own Maya-and-Lee example: a look at an unexplored cell rarely flips the current best answer in one step, so V(a) is near zero and the added λ term does all the exploration. Honest wording would be "myopic value of information per cost, as a heuristic". The guarantees do not transfer either. They need a finite hypothesis set, known likelihoods for every test outcome, and (mostly) conditionally independent tests **[unverified beyond the abstracts]**; a vision-language model looking at video has unknown and miscalibrated likelihoods, an open `OTHER` hypothesis, correlated repeat looks and false positives. EC²'s "correlated noise" is correlation with a known joint model, and the same group later wrote that for general noisy tests "no efficient policies are known to be provably competitive with the optimal policy" ([arXiv 1605.07334](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07334)).

**The residual-mass rule is classical optimal search theory and should be cited as such.** For a target hidden in one of n boxes with a known detection probability and search time per box, "an optimal search strategy, first discovered by Blackwell (reported in Matula (1964)), is to always next search a box with a maximal probability of detection per unit time at that moment", with the location belief updated by Bayes' theorem after each unsuccessful look ([Clarkson, Lin, Glazebrook, arXiv 2103.09310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09310)). Koopman's three 1956 Operations Research papers and Stone's "Theory of Optimal Search" (1975) are the foundation ([arXiv 2206.06450](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06450)). The proposal's λ·Σ m(g)·P_detect ÷ cost is that index summed over the cells one look covers; `update_residual_mass` is the Bayes update after a miss; the requirement that activity cues "cannot zero out" the prior is the standard full-support condition; and the `ABSENT` state is what land search-and-rescue calls "rest of the world", the cell that receives no search and "will necessarily grow larger" as the search proceeds ([Judgment and Decision Making](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/EF44B5DEF980D0B8ADE23620AFA467B4/S1930297500002825a.pdf/cognition-in-the-woods-biases-in-probability-judgments-by-search-and-rescue-planners.pdf)). The proposal's second method, Residual-Mass Temporal Search, is discrete Bayesian search almost exactly. The novelty claim for an "explicit unseen-evidence state and miss-risk model" should read "optimal search theory applied to video with heterogeneous model-based sensors". The classical rule is exactly optimal only for minimising expected cost when the target is certainly present; with a budget and a possibly absent target it is a heuristic that needs a stopping rule **[the Kadane, Chew and Ross references for this are recalled, unverified]**.

**The λ bonus double counts.** If the outcome model behind V(a) includes "new candidate found in this cell", the value of finding evidence is counted once in V(a) and again in the λ term. A single Bayesian model has one posterior and no separate bonus. As written, the ablation "RRES without the residual-mass exploration term" is confounded. The fix is to drop either the "new candidate" outcome or λ and let the search index be the only driver of exploration.

## Novelty: the certificate is open, the rest is taken or contested

The proposal's related work is out of date on three papers. **CASE** (arXiv 2610.05400, 4 October 2026) is a plug-in stopping rule for long-video agents: a Ridge regressor over 18 features, including option entropy, consumed tokens and five tool-type indicators, predicts the gap between answering now and continuing, "accounting jointly for answer correctness and the full cost of continued reasoning", and cuts model tokens by about 54.6% on average. It makes the proposal's own argument: "confidence, entropy, or agreement signals summarize current certainty rather than the value of another search step" ([arXiv 2610.05400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05400)). **ForeSeek** (arXiv 2611.26024, 19 November 2026) keeps a "global interval belief mechanism that iteratively verifies and updates predictions over unseen regions, turning failed predictions into negative evidence" ([arXiv 2611.26024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26024)). **VESTA** (arXiv 2608.31005, 31 August 2026) keeps a "temporal evidence ledger" that "merges inspected intervals to estimate temporal coverage" and records hypothesis support, and states its own limits: recall retrieval "does not guarantee recovery of every relevant occurrence" and "finalization remains a Reasoner decision rather than a programmatic verification gate" ([arXiv 2608.31005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31005)). All three evaluate on multiple-choice benchmarks only, where "not present" can never be the right answer.

The defensible paper is the proposal's own fallback: search coverage and evidence absence. That means a detection model that converts logged coverage into miss probability, a rule for "not present", and an evaluation on evidence-absent long-video questions, which no agent paper found has run. The window is short. ForeSeek, ActiveForager, SAVE and CASE all appeared within seven weeks, and each is one step away: ForeSeek adding a stop rule on its beliefs, CASE adding an abstain action, VESTA adding a gate on its coverage view. No paper was found that applies classical search theory or conformal calibration to a video-search agent, but neither was searched for specifically, so this is weak evidence.

## Absence is the hard part: one clean pass still leaves 23% missed

The proposal allows "not present" when "the modeled probability of a missed matching event" falls below a threshold. The arithmetic of that rule is unforgiving. Take a prior probability of absence of 0.25, the proposal's share of absent queries, and one look at every cell with detection probability d. After no detection, the probability that the evidence is present but was missed is (1 − p₀)(1 − d) ÷ (p₀ + (1 − p₀)(1 − d)). **At d = 0.9 that is 23%.** A threshold of 5% needs d of about 0.98 in one pass, or two independent passes at 0.9 (about 3%). If the assumed d is 0.9 and the true d is 0.6, the true miss risk is 55%; at 0.3 it is 68%. An optimistic detection probability makes the certificate wrong in the unsafe direction, and nothing inside the run reveals it. (These are our derivations from Bayes' rule.)

**Repeat looks by a model are not independent.** The factor (1 − d)ᵏ for k looks assumes independent glimpses. A vision-language model shown the same frames twice returns nearly the same answer, so a second identical look adds almost nothing, while a likelihood product counts it in full. `design.md` already says this in section 4 ("A second look by the same model can repeat the same mistake"). The practical rule is one credit per cell, instrument and density, and a discounted credit for a different instrument after a miss, because instances that are hard for one sensor tend to be hard for the others.

**False positives need a confirming look.** The basic search model has no false alarms; a model does report things that are not there. A detection is then not conclusive: with false-alarm rate f per look, the probability that a positive is real is m·d ÷ (m·d + (1 − m)·f). The classical remedy is a confirmation step, here a look by a different instrument or at higher resolution before a detection counts. Asking the same model whether its own cited frame supports its claim does not work: in one study it caught 0% of 40 fabricated claims, where a small entailment verifier caught 79% ([arXiv 2608.15574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15574), abstract only).

**Search theory itself warns that the plan optimises the analyst's model, not the truth.** A uniformly optimal plan maximises subjective detection probability and "does not necessarily maximize the true detection probability" ([arXiv 2601.00350](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00350v1)); when the modelled possibility area does not contain the target, true detection probability is zero ([arXiv 2311.18226](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18226v4)). The video analogue is a misread question or evidence of a kind no instrument can see. The Air France 447 search is the worked case: the wreckage was found in 2011 only after analysts allowed that the acoustic beacons had failed, which lowered the assumed detection probability of an earlier search and moved probability back into an area already "searched" ([Stone et al., arXiv 1405.4720](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4720)). The consequence for RRES is that thresholds must not be read as probabilities. They should be set on held-out, source-disjoint queries so that the observed rate of wrong committed outputs is controlled, by selective classification with guaranteed risk (Geifman and El-Yaniv, NeurIPS 2017) or conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., ICLR 2024) ([arXiv 1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500); [arXiv 2208.02814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814)). The exact conformal threshold formula and its exchangeability condition are recalled **[unverified]**. Such a guarantee holds only for queries like the calibration ones, and with 12–20 source videos the effective sample size is closer to the number of videos than the number of queries.

**Thirty absent queries cannot establish a 5% rate.** The proposal's gate requires absent-evidence false positives of "5% or below" on about 30 absent queries. With zero errors in 30 trials, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the true rate is about 9.5%; with one error it is about 15%. **About 60 error-free trials are needed** for a 5% upper bound, before any are set aside for calibration (our arithmetic).

**Models fail in both directions.** Unassisted models over-answer: across 22 video models in RevisitQA, median abstention on unanswerable items was 30.3% and 19 of 22 abstained on fewer than half ([arXiv 2609.30981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30981)); in AnswerPool, "seven of eight open-weight models answer 87 to 100% of unanswerable questions" ([arXiv 2609.37494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37494)). But once an "unanswerable" option is offered inside an agent framework, strong models over-abstain: in SuperMemory-VQA "a major failure mode on answerable questions is not choosing the wrong answer, but abstaining when evidence is present", with Gemini-3-Flash wrongly abstaining on 39.9% of answerable cases and several open-source models on more than 70% ([arXiv 2606.00825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00825)). The proposal lists "unnecessary abstention on answerable queries" as a secondary report. It has to be a primary metric, and any gain on absence must be shown at matched coverage of answerable questions. Because several studies find that models perceive the gap but fail to act on it, a harness rule that decides from coverage state, not from the model's self-report, is well motivated; that is the choice `design.md` already made. No paper found reports the absent-evidence false-positive rate of any leading search agent, so UberLens would have to measure it.

## The experiment cannot support its gates as written

The design has real strengths: grounded success as the primary metric, source-disjoint splits, a sweep over budgets, a charged-versus-free reasoning ablation, and an offline oracle bound on action value that would stop the work early if search has no leverage. Five problems remain.

The absence gate fails on sample size, as shown above. The stratum requirement fails the same way: the gate asks for "a clear advantage on near-duplicate/brief-evidence strata", which hold 30 and 20 queries, and `design.md` section 8.2 already notes that one task moves a 20-task score by five points. The abandon threshold of "under roughly 3 points" is below what 120 queries resolve; an unpaired proportion near 50% at n = 120 has a standard error of about 4.6 points (our arithmetic), and pairing helps only as far as the two systems agree. The paired bootstrap is clustered over 12–20 source videos, fewer once calibration videos are removed.

The controls are underspecified where it matters. The "simple active explorer" must be SAVE proper plus a one-query variant. The query-aware retrieval baseline rests on a whole-video retrieval citation, and the within-video evidence puts it near uniform sampling.

The ablations are confounded by the λ double count, and the "generic answer-confidence stopping" ablation needs CASE as the comparison, since CASE is the published cost-aware stopping rule.

The detection probabilities that the whole method depends on have no source. The proposal estimates them from "hand-coded sensor semantics plus calibration frequencies". No paper found measures how often a look by a given instrument at a given density catches an annotated event. Measuring one stratum to ±0.08–0.11 takes 50–100 labelled positives (binomial interval at d ≈ 0.8, our arithmetic), and the calibration split of a 120-query set cannot fill a table of evidence type by instrument by density.

Absent queries need a construction. "This does not happen anywhere in a 90-minute video" is expensive to certify by annotation, and the proposal does not say how it will be done.

## Benchmarks: none fits whole, four are worth using

No benchmark found combines long videos, evidence absent from the whole video, labelled spans for answerable items and a redistributable licence.

**LV-Haystack** is usable through LongVideoBench. Haystack-LVBench has 114 videos, 342 questions and 496 labelled keyframes on videos averaging 14.1 minutes, and is produced by a 90-line script in the T* repository from LongVideoBench's validation file, so it needs no Hugging Face access; Haystack-Ego4D has 988 videos and 15,092 questions ([arXiv HTML, Table 1, App. E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02259); [GitHub](https://github.com/mll-lab-nu/TStar)). **Temporal recall is the usable metric**: recall at 32 frames separates methods (13.8 to 26.3 on Ego4D), while F1 sits at 1.6–3.4 for everything and the SSIM-based visual scores give uniform sampling about 60. For UberLens the natural form is "did a cited look include a decoded timestamp within ±5 s of each labelled keyframe". The limits: labels are points, not spans; subtitle questions were removed; there are no scored absent cases; and the videos are Ego4D and LongVideoBench, neither redistributable (LongVideoBench is CC-BY-NC-SA per a search snippet; **the LV-Haystack card itself is unverified**). It belongs in `design.md` section 8.6, through a download adapter.

**AgentVidBench** (arXiv 2609.21386) is the only benchmark found whose video files are confirmed openly licensed: 100 hand-written multi-hop questions over 71 videos of up to 30 minutes, with step-by-step milestones, and the authors "manually verified that its Creative Commons license or public-domain status allows us to include the video file in the benchmark package" ([arXiv 2609.21386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21386)). It is small and has no unanswerable items, and its annotation licence was not found. Its source list is a ready pool of footage for UberLens Bench.

**CG-Bench** (arXiv 2412.12075) is the largest with labelled evidence: 1,219 long videos, 12,129 question-answer-clue triplets and 14,362 human-annotated clue intervals averaging 19.24 s, with an accuracy-at-IoU metric that is grounded success under another name ([arXiv 2412.12075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12075)). Its dataset licence is unverified. **SuperMemory-VQA** (arXiv 2606.00825) is the closest on content: 52.9 hours of egocentric video, 4,853 questions each with an explicit "unanswerable" option, evidence timestamps, and an Ans-F1 metric for the answerable-or-not decision, to be released under CC BY-NC ([arXiv 2606.00825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00825)). Both are for comparison only. VirtueBench is less useful than it looks: its "unanswerable" means the key frame was missing from a fixed sample, which an agent that can fetch more frames defeats ([arXiv 2603.07071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07071)).

**Exact absence can be built by cutting the labelled span out.** Take an item with a labelled evidence span and remove that span from the file; the original question becomes evidence-absent by construction, and the uncut file is its matched present case. No cited paper does this (NOAH does the inverse and inserts clips); it is our suggestion, and it fits the "ground truth from code" principle in `design.md` section 8.1. Two checks are needed, both our inference: a second instance of the evidence must not remain elsewhere in the source, and the splice must not give itself away, either to the model as a visible jump or to the activity profile as a forced keyframe.

## Changes to design.md

### (a) Adopt now: cheap, and consistent with the current design

1. **Log a residual-miss estimate with every answer (sections 4 and 5.4).** Compute it from the existing coverage log; do not change search. Keep one weight per timeline cell and one for "absent", multiply a cell's weight by (1 − d) once per instrument and density that looked at it, and report 1 − w(absent) ÷ Σw. The detection value d comes from a small hand-set table by instrument and claim kind, lowered when `max_gap` exceeds the length of the event being sought, since a look at one frame per 25 s cannot be credited with catching a three-second event. Give no credit for a repeat of the same look. Label it a score, not a probability, and say the table is hand-set.
2. **Make the outcome three-way and score it (sections 5.5 and 8.2).** Every question ends as `answer`, `not enough evidence`, or `not seen at this coverage`. The third keeps the current wording; no claim of "not present" is added. Report false answers on absent items and **unnecessary abstention on answerable items** as primary metrics, with absence results at matched coverage of answerable questions.
3. **Add absent-evidence and near-duplicate strata to Find and Answer, sized for the gate (section 8.2).** Build absence by cutting the labelled span out, with the uncut file as the matched present case and the two checks named above. A gate of "5% or below" needs about 60 error-free absent test cases; with fewer, state the observed rate with its interval and call it that.
4. **Name SAVE proper and a one-query variant as controls (section 8.3).** Range query, up to 30 queries, at most 40 frames per query, resolution by span width, the published prompt; written from the paper, since no code or licence is confirmed. Add native full-video inference as a reference point where the model supports it: SAVE found no gain from exploring under five minutes.
5. **Give `inspect` a harness-default frame count by span width, with the agent's override as an ablation (sections 5.3 and 8.3).** SAVE's values are 6 frames for spans up to 10 s, 12 up to 60 s, 25 up to 8 minutes. This tests SAVE's finding that harness-set density beat model-chosen density against UberLens's logged agent choice.
6. **Charge per-step output tokens (sections 4 and 8.1).** Split input, output and thinking tokens in the budget and the cost report, with preprocessing amortised per source reported separately, so results can be set beside SAVE's dollars.
7. **Add LV-Haystack and AgentVidBench to the external benchmarks (sections 8.6, 12, 13).** LV-Haystack via the LongVideoBench script, scored by temporal recall at ±5 s on cited looks; write "LongVideoBench subset", never "LVBench subset". AgentVidBench as the one redistributable comparison and as candidate footage for open question 2. List the LV-Haystack card, CG-Bench and AgentVidBench annotation licences as unverified.
8. **Cite optimal search theory where coverage and absence are defined (sections 4 and 5.4).** Koopman (1956), Blackwell/Matula (1964), Black (1965), Stone (1975), and the Air France 447 case for what a wrong detection assumption does. Note in section 12 that the VideoAgent cited in this research is Stanford's, not the HKUDS project.

### (b) Experiments, each behind a flag

9. **Residual-mass-guided next look.** Score candidate looks by m·d ÷ cost, with no λ bonus and one credit per cell, instrument and density; require a confirming look by a different instrument before a detection counts. Compare it with the default policy and with ForeSeek's belief scheduler under question 3 of section 8.5: it is the probabilistic form of the same idea.
10. **Thresholds set on held-out data.** Run each trajectory to the full budget, fix the stop point afterwards by the threshold, and choose the threshold on source-disjoint queries by selective risk control or conformal risk control. Only under this flag may the harness upgrade "not seen at this coverage" to "not present", and only with the calibrated error rate published beside it.
11. **Measured detection tables.** Replace the hand-set values with hit rates per instrument, density and claim kind, false-alarm rates on spans known to be empty, and the rate at which a second instrument detects after a first has missed, all from logged looks against labelled spans; 50–100 positives per stratum that matters.
12. **An offline oracle bound on action value.** From logged runs and labels, compute which allowed look would have helped most at each step. If an oracle selector does not beat the default at the same budget, stop work on policies.
13. **Answer-hypothesis posteriors and value-of-information scoring, last.** This is the part most exposed to prior art (VideoHV-Agent, VESTA, CASE) and to SAVE's cost finding. Run it only if items 9–12 show leverage, with reasoning charged and CASE as the stopping baseline.

### (c) Not adopting, and why

14. **`absence` as a supportable claim in the default harness.** One clean pass at d = 0.9 leaves 23% missed, repeat looks are correlated, and the detection values are unmeasured. The row in section 4 stays as written.
15. **The λ bonus added to V(a).** It double counts and confounds its own ablation.
16. **A model-reported "calibrated belief" as the stop rule.** AVP's thresholds are nearly flat, models over-answer and over-abstain, and the store's kind-based check already replaces self-report.
17. **Model-compiled evidence obligations.** The claim-kind table is a fixed, checkable form of the same idea; a free-form obligation graph adds the decomposition errors the proposal itself names and per-step tokens.
18. **The 120-query go/no-go gate as written.** Its absence threshold, stratum claims and 3-point abandon rule are below the resolution of the sample.
19. **Code from AVP (CC BY-NC) or VideoHV-Agent (no LICENSE file), and a dense embedding index on the strength of the 1.9% figure.** The query-aware scan stays in section 11 behind its budget gate.

### Order of work

Run the cheapest falsifying test first. Keep the default search, add item 1, build matched absent and present cases by item 3, and ask one question: **does thresholding the residual-miss estimate separate true-absent cases from missed-present cases better than the model's own confidence?** The two populations are questions where the evidence was cut out and questions where it is present but the run found nothing. If the estimate does not separate them better than confidence, the certificate claim fails before any policy is built, and items 9, 10 and 13 are dropped. If it does, measure the detection tables (11), run the oracle bound (12), then the guided search (9) and calibrated thresholds (10). The hypothesis posterior (13) is last.

## Conclusion

The proposal reached for active hypothesis testing and landed on a 1960s search problem. That reframing is the useful result: the question "can the agent say the thing is not there?" has a classical model, a classical index rule and seventy years of evidence about how it fails, chiefly an optimistic detection probability that nobody can see from inside the search. UberLens is unusually well placed to test it, because its observations already record what search theory needs and most video agents discard: which instrument looked, at which decoded timestamps, with what largest gap.

The larger shift is in what counts as the contribution. The field's agents are all scored on multiple-choice questions where absence cannot be right, so nobody knows how often they invent an answer. A benchmark stratum with exact absence, a three-way outcome, and an estimate that is honest about being a score would measure something no published agent paper has measured, without adding a single step of deliberation to the search loop. The policy that the proposal is named for is the least certain part of it and can wait for that measurement.
